Transport an exceptional-set estimate through a Collatz endpoint

A typical Collatz orbit contracts over one logarithmic block. Repeating that observation requires controlling the arithmetically generated landing values. This paper isolates that problem as a reusable theorem about arbitrary target sets.

Use the shortcut map \(T(n)=n/2\) for even \(n\), and \(T(n)=(3n+1)/2\) for odd \(n\). Its block-endpoint map is

\[F(n)=T^{\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor}(n).\]

Call a target set \(S\) \((C,D)\)-dense when the count of positive integers outside \(S\) up to \(X\) is at most \(CX^{1-D}\), in the range specified in the paper. The pullback consists of starts whose endpoint belongs to \(S\).

Theorem 5.3 · arbitrary-target endpoint transport

For fixed admissible parameters, small positive \(D\), and any \((C,D)\)-dense target, the pullback has density parameters of the form

\[(C,D)\longmapsto \left(K(1+C^{\theta/(1+\theta)})D^{-\theta/(2(1+\theta))},\ \chi D\right).\]

Here \(1/2<\theta<1\), \(0<\chi<a_0\theta/(1+\theta)\), and \(a_0=(\log_2 3)/2\). After the auxiliary parameters are fixed, the constant \(K\) and the admissible cutoff for \(D\) are independent of \(C,D,S\) and later iterations.

The output density exponent is asymptotically linear in the input exponent. Version 2.2.0 also improves the prefactor: its dependence on \(C\) is sublinear, and its dependence on \(D^{-1}\) is the explicit power displayed above. These details matter when the operator is applied repeatedly.

The natural-density descent application

Put

\[\delta_0=\frac{\log(1/a_0)}{\log(2/a_0)}=0.251245530155874\ldots, \qquad c_*=\frac2{\log(4/3)}.\]

For every fixed \(0<\delta<\delta_0\), every \(0<\sigma<1-\delta/\delta_0\), and every \(c>c_*\), Theorem 1.1 bounds the number of starts \(n\le X\) with no witness satisfying

\[k<c\log n,\qquad T^k(n)\le\exp((\log n)^{1-\delta})\]

by \(5X\exp(-a_{\delta,\sigma,c}(\log X)^\sigma)\), for all sufficiently large \(X\). In particular, those witnesses exist on a set of natural density one. The choice \(c=6.953\) is admissible.

The endpoint \(\delta=\delta_0\) is not included. The result is asymptotic, permits exceptional orbits, and does not establish the Collatz conjecture.

From endpoint fibers to repeated descent

  1. Parity coordinates. A finite parity word gives an exact affine expression for the block endpoint.
  2. Fixed-total compositions. These coordinates organize how many starting values can reach a given endpoint.
  3. A Rényi moment estimate. The estimate controls concentration in the endpoint fibers.
  4. Hölder's inequality. Combining the fiber estimate with the mass of an arbitrary bad target gives the transport theorem.
  5. Endpoint-only iteration. The operator can now be applied repeatedly without assuming uniform landing values; balancing contraction against density loss gives the stated \(\delta\)-range.

The later polylogarithmic-descent paper uses an independent first-passage proof and reaches a smaller target. CET remains a separate operator theorem for arbitrary targets; its role is broader than the particular stretched-logarithmic application.
